The problem

Ordered here, paid elsewhere.

Merchants submitted orders on Lightspeed’s wholesale platform, but supplier payment happened elsewhere. The legacy experience was a single payments page that tried to serve both merchants and suppliers, and failed both. Usability issues and confusing messaging eroded merchant confidence. Fragmented and manual workflows failed to support how wholesale suppliers get paid.

15%of suppliers had payments enabled. Payments was part of Lightspeed’s broader strategy, and every off-platform transaction meant missed revenue for the business.
Reframing the problem

Checkout was only half the problem.

A single wholesale order often mixes items paid today with items paid after delivery, and nothing guided merchants or suppliers through that. I reframed the initiative as an opportunity to learn directly from our users, using checkout as a lens to uncover new insights and challenge old assumptions. This shifted the focus from fixing checkout to designing a scalable end-to-end payments experience.

Discovery & Alignment

Design the happy path. Learn directly from users.

Moderated tests with 15 ICP users revealed what we could ship, what needed iteration, and where further discovery was needed. When GTM pushed to solve every edge case up front, I advocated for a pragmatic path: ship an MVP checkout, instrument it with tracking, and use real user data to inform future iterations.

Key insights
Payment follows fulfillmentThe platform was built to charge against orders, but merchants are charged per delivery as products are fulfilled. One order can become several deliveries, so merchants needed clarity on what payment was due and when.
Merchants verify before they commitMerchants use checkout to review and confirm their order. They valued detail and clear communication, and needed to see exactly what they were agreeing to before they submitted.
Invoices are their own workflowMerchants needed to know what they owed, and suppliers needed to get paid on time across multiple orders and deliveries. Invoices needed their own screen and an efficient way to manage them.
The solution

From a payments page to a payments platform.

I collaborated with product and engineering partners to translate the vision into actionable next steps. Balancing long-term strategy with the need for rapid MVP delivery, we shaped an executive-backed, 18-month roadmap to connect checkout and invoicing into one comprehensive experience. The shipped solution delivers an end-to-end experience where merchants can confidently self-serve, and suppliers get paid on platform and on time.

Design decisionPayment follows fulfillmentOne order can ship in multiple deliveries, with payment due at different times. I broke deliveries out so merchants can verify what ships and what they’ll owe.
TradeoffRead-only address in v1Inline editing is the norm at checkout, but the address is set upstream. I kept it visible and read-only in v1 so merchants could still catch an error before submitting.
Design decisionClarity after checkoutThe confirmation shows what’s due for each delivery, so merchants can see how payment will follow fulfillment after placing one order.

Results & Reflection

A foundation for B2B payments.

In its first year, the new experience increased supplier adoption by 24% and grew payment volume by 5×, establishing itself as a reliable revenue driver for Lightspeed’s business growth. I designed it as a foundation, built to be iterated on as we learned from users and as adjacent and supporting work unlocked new functionality and opportunity for collaboration. Additionally, my solution introduced reusable components and patterns setting the foundation for scalable experiences across the product ecosystem.
