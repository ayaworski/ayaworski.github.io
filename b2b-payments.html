The problem

Ordered here, paid elsewhere.

Merchants submitted orders on Lightspeed’s wholesale platform, but payment often happened elsewhere. The legacy experience was a single payments page that tried to serve both merchants and suppliers, and failed both. Usability issues and confusing messaging eroded merchant confidence. Fragmented and manual workflows failed to support how wholesale suppliers get paid.

Payments was part of Lightspeed’s broader growth strategy, and every off-platform transaction meant missed revenue for the business.

3.8%Only 153 of 4000 eligible suppliers had payments enabled.
Reframing the problem

Payment happens at multiple touch points.

A typical wholesale order often mixes products paid immediately with products paid after future deliveries. Using checkout as a lens to uncover real operation workflows and challenge old platform assumptions, I reframed this initiative as an opportunity to learn directly from our users. This shifted the focus from fixing a payments screen to designing a comprehensive payments experience.

Key insights
Merchants verify before they commitMerchants use checkout to review and confirm their order. They valued detail and clear communication, and needed to see exactly what they were agreeing to before they submitted.
Invoices are their own workflowMerchants needed to know what they owed, and suppliers needed to be paid on time. Across many orders and deliveries, invoices needed their own screen and an efficient way to manage them.
Payment follows fulfillmentThe platform charged per order, but merchants are charged per delivery as products are fulfilled. One order can become several deliveries, so merchants needed clarity on what payment was due and when.
The solution

From a payments page to a payments platform.

Balancing long-term strategy with the need for rapid delivery, I connected checkout and invoicing into one comprehensive payments experience. Merchants can confidently self-serve, while suppliers get paid on platform and on time.

Results & Reflection

A platform-first solution for B2B payments.

Four months after launch, 24% more suppliers had signed up for our B2B payments solution. By the end of the first year, payment volume across the wholesale network had grown 5×, from $3.5M to $17.5M.

Upstream dependencies kept me from shipping the ideal experience at launch, so I built for what they would eventually allow. As those dependencies cleared and we learned how merchants and suppliers used it, we added shipping, tax and multicurrency support across the checkout and invoice experience. The 18-month roadmap kept the door open for additional functionality including editable invoices, payment links and invoice automation.

What I learned: Ship what the constraints allow, but design for the North Star version so the team has something to build towards. Treating the upstream limits as temporary let me hold the ideal experience in the structure of the solution until the dependencies caught up, and real usage then showed us which additions mattered most.
