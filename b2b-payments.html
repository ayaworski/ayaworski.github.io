The problem

Ordered here, paid elsewhere.

Merchants ordered wholesale from suppliers on Lightspeed’s B2B marketplace, but payment happened somewhere else. The legacy payments experience was fragmented and manual, limiting supplier adoption and constraining business growth. Low automation, limited payment methods, and an outdated UI eroded trust and blocked growth.

15%of suppliers had payments enabled. Payments was part of Lightspeed’s broader strategy, and every off-platform order meant missed revenue from payment fees.
Reframing the problem

From payments page to payments platform.

I reframed the challenge as an opportunity to learn directly from our users. Instead of focusing on immediate delivery, I used checkout as a lens to uncover opportunities, validate assumptions, and shape long-term product direction, shifting the focus from fixing a checkout to designing a scalable end-to-end payments experience.

Discovery & Alignment

Design the happy path. Learn directly from users.

Moderated testing provided deep insights into what users did and why, allowing us to validate against our hypotheses. Testing revealed where we could iterate quickly, where clarity allowed us to plan confidently, and where further investigation was needed.

When product partners pushed to solve every edge case up front, I advocated for a pragmatic approach: launch an MVP checkout experience, instrument it with tracking, and use real user data to inform future iterations.

93%noticed and understood multiple payment methods.
67%understood when payment would be processed.
Key insightsKept upstream details lockedSome merchants wanted to edit delivery location and shipping method at checkout. The existing platform and workflows set those upstream, so I kept them read-only and focused checkout on review and payment.
Cut deferred-payment messagingI dropped “payment due after shipping” messaging for preseason orders, and a paid-today vs. paid-later breakdown for mixed orders, in favour of requesting a payment method upfront.
Reframed the goal with GTMGTM saw payments as a way for suppliers to collect. I shifted the focus to merchants self-serving across the end-to-end experience, not a single screen, and away from building for edge cases.
The solution

One flow, order to paid.

The new experience connects checkout and invoicing in one flow. Merchants can self-serve, and suppliers get paid on platform and on time.

Measurable Impact

B2B payments, from 0 → 1.

In its first year, the new payments experience grew transaction volume and increased self-service adoption. I designed it as a foundation, built to be iterated on as we learned from users and as adjacent and supporting work unlocked new functionality.
