The problem

Retailers juggled multiple brand portals to restock one store.

The legacy wholesale model was a closed network. Merchants could only buy from brands they already worked with, logging into separate brand portals and juggling multiple carts to place orders. This siloed experience limited organic discovery, constrained new supplier–merchant relationships and hindered the growth potential of the B2B network. Lightspeed saw an opportunity in opening that network, betting that organic discovery would fuel new relationships on both sides.

Reframing the Problem

Wholesale runs on relationships, not shopping carts.

The initial ask was ambiguous, open up the wholesale network. During discovery, I noticed fellow designers framing the problem as a typical consumer experience. But wholesale operates differently. Brands choose who they sell to, and pricing and availability are set based on relationship and trust. I reframed the work around two core goals: designing a marketplace that supports existing brand–merchant relationships and creating a space that helps to establish new ones.

Key insights
Discovery drives new relationshipsDiscovery is the key to promoting new supplier–merchant relationships. Products and brands needed to be discoverable across the marketplace so buyers could find suppliers they didn’t already know.
Suppliers need their own spaceSuppliers still need a place to showcase their brand identity and the products they sell, so they could appeal to the right customers in the right way.
Suppliers control the relationshipSuppliers decide who they sell to and at what price, so the marketplace had to open up discovery without taking that control away from brands.
The solution

A wholesale network built to grow.

I shipped a marketplace MVP that opens a closed wholesale network to discovery. Brands reach merchants beyond their existing relationships, while merchants find new suppliers that fit their store, strengthening engagement across the network.

Results & Reflection

Shipped in five months, but unproven.

I took the marketplace from concept to launch in five months. Lightspeed unveiled the multi-brand experience at NRF’s Big Show in January 2026 as a foundational step toward connecting its wholesale supplier channel to its retail POS product.

My solution opened the network to discovery, but an open network was only half the bet. NuORDER had been built on private portals, and most suppliers were set up to sell only to existing connections. The brands that did fit an open model still needed to be found and onboarded. I moved to another project shortly after launch, so I can’t speak to the long-term results.

What I learned: A marketplace is two-sided, and the supplier side is often the harder design problem. The insight that brands control the relationship shaped the experience, and also predicted the adoption challenge: brands had to choose to open up.

Next time, I’d push for two things before launch. First, a clear definition of success: new merchant–brand relationships that lead to an order. Second, supplier onboarding designed into the product, not left to a separate rollout.
